## Documented incident timelines

**M/V Marathassa, English Bay, Vancouver, April 8–9, 2015** (fuel oil, ≈2,800 L on the water).
Source: Butler, *Independent Review of the M/V Marathassa Fuel Oil Spill Environmental Response Operation*, July 2015,
https://waves-vagues.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/library-bibliotheque/4109461x.pdf

| Event | Time | After report |
|---|---|---|
| Sheen reported to the Coast Guard | 16:48 | 0 |
| Port boat on scene, sorbent pads | 17:30–17:40 | ~0.7 h |
| Coast Guard gives the response organization (WCMRC) a heads-up | 18:08 | 1 h 20 min |
| WCMRC formally activated | 19:57 | 3 h 09 min |
| WCMRC on scene, skimming | 21:25 | ≈4 h 37 min |
| Boom around the source ship completed | 05:53 next day (05:25 in the executive summary) | ≈13 h |

- Butler calls the response "well within the established standards." The Canadian standard in a designated port is 6 h.
- The review blames a 1 h 49 min delay on confusion over who was in charge.
- The weather was near ideal.
- About 1,400 L of the oil was recovered.
- **Secondary:** the City of Vancouver was not alerted until 6 a.m. the next day (Global News).

**Aleutian Isle, west side of San Juan Island, August 13, 2022** (diesel, about 2,500 gal on board)
- Washington Ecology was notified at 5:00 p.m.
- Unified Command was activated at 9 a.m. the next day.
- At 1 p.m. the next day, two response contractors were still "en route."
- The first sorbent boom was reported in the 3:30 p.m. update, about 22 h after notification.
- 3,800 ft of boom was deployed by August 16.
- The sheen was more than 2 miles long and entered Canadian waters.

Sources: https://ecology.wa.gov/spills-cleanup/spills/spill-preparedness-response/responding-to-spill-incidents/spill-incidents/aleutian-isle-sinking ·
https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCG/bulletins/3281128 ·
https://clearseas.org/wp-content/uploads/SSTWG-2022-Transboundary-Oil-Spill-Aleutian-Isle-Case-Study.pdf

**ARCO Anchorage, Port Angeles Harbor, December 21, 1985** (ANS crude, 5,690 bbl).
The ship grounded at 16:26. Cargo transfer stopped the discharge at 20:52. The response team was flown in from Long Beach,
California. No boom time is published. https://incidentnews.noaa.gov/incident/6319

**Nathan E. Stewart, near Bella Bella, BC, October 13, 2016** (diesel, ≈110,000 L; outside the Salish Sea; **secondary**)
- The first boom was about 10 h after the spill, and strong currents pulled it apart.
- Response vessels took 17 h; WCMRC came from Prince Rupert, more than 20 h by water.
- The Transportation Safety Board (TSB) found the prescribed time standards were met.

Sources: Clear Seas, Sightline (citing The Tyee, Globe and Mail, Vancouver Sun), Heiltsuk report summary.

**Nestucca, 1988** (≈5,500 bbl heavy fuel oil).
Rough seas defeated the first response. The submerged oil could not be seen from the air until it came ashore ~100 miles
north. https://ecology.wa.gov/blog/december-2020/the-nestucca

**Exxon Valdez, 1989.**
The contingency barge "was not loaded with oil spill response equipment" (NTSB, https://ntsb.gov/safety/safety-recs/recletters/M90_50_52.pdf).
"Skimmers were not readily available during the first 24 hours" (EPA).

## Planning standards (hours from notification)

Washington rules are in WAC 173-182: https://app.leg.wa.gov/wac/default.aspx?cite=173-182&full=true. Washington says these
"do not constitute cleanup standards."

| Rule | Standard |
|---|---|
| -370 San Juan County | 2 h: work boat + 1,000 ft boom · 3 h: +2,000 ft · 4 h: +200 ft, 196 bbl storage · 6 h: +10,000 ft, recovery 3% of WCS or 12,500 bbl/day · 12 h: +20,000 ft, 10% or 36,000 · 24 h: +20,000 ft, 14% or 48,000 · 48 h: 25% or 60,000 bbl/day. 2 h and 3 h resources must be resident |
| -375 Padilla Bay | 1.5 h: 1,000 ft · 2 h: +2,000 ft · 6 h: +10,000 ft, 3% or 12,500 bbl (half in water ≤ 10 ft deep) |
| -390 Dungeness | 2 h: 1,000 ft · 3 h: +2,000 ft · 6 h: +7,000 ft (≥ 3,000 ft open-water boom) |
| -395 Neah Bay | 2 h: 1,000 ft · 3 h: 2,000 ft open-water boom · 6 h: +6,000 ft (≥ 4,000 ft open-water) |
| -355 transfer sites / terminals | 2 h: 1,000 ft · 6 h: +10,000 ft, 10% or 12,500 bbl · 12 h: 15% or 36,000 · 24 h: 20% or 48,000 · 48 h: 25% or 60,000 |
| -321 / -320 aerial surveillance | On scene in 6 h (vessels and facilities) |
| -325 / -330 dispersants / burning | On scene in 12 h, where allowed |
| -522 shoreline | 100 trained workers within 24 h |
| -540 wildlife | 12 h: deterrence and field stabilization · 24 h: 9 field staff and a rehab facility |

- The Washington rule is being revised. A preliminary draft (Aug 2026) touches -320, -321, -522 and -540.

Federal rules (33 CFR 155, https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/33/155.1050):
- Higher-volume port areas include the Strait of Juan de Fuca and Puget Sound.
- **Worst case discharge:** resources in tiers at 12 / 36 / 60 h; elsewhere inland 24 / 48 / 72 h.
- **Maximum most probable discharge:** resources required within 12 h.
- **Average most probable discharge** (cargo transfers): boom twice the vessel length within 1 h.

Canada (TP 12401, https://tc.canada.ca/en/marine-transportation/publications/tp-12401-response-organizations-standards):
- 150 t deployed in 6 h in a designated port (Vancouver).
- 1,000 t in 12 h, 2,500 t in 18 h, 10,000 t in 72 h.
- Outside those areas, add travel time at 6 knots.

WCMRC's Trans Mountain enhancement (https://wcmrc.com/?p=3651):
- 2 h in the designated port and 6 h in the southern shipping lanes, down from 72 h.
- Bases at Beecher Bay, Nanaimo, Port Alberni, Sidney and others.

## Effectiveness and weather limits

- **ITOPF:** usually only 10–15% of spilled oil is recovered at sea, and "rarely is more than 10%." Booms cannot hold oil
  against a cross-current over 0.7 knots. https://www.itopf.org/knowledge-resources/documents-guides/response-techniques/containment-recovery/
- **Georgia Strait Alliance intervenor evidence (2018):** conditions where response is impossible occur 6–9% of the time at
  New Dungeness, 30–40% at Neah Bay and 43–49% at La Perouse Bank. Currents in Race Passage routinely exceed boom limits.
  https://georgiastrait.org/a96417-3-appendix-a-limits-to-effectiveness-of-containment-booms-in-the-project-marine-area-a6l5i1
- **Nuka Research 2015** (secondary summary only): the response gap in the Strait of Juan de Fuca is 49% overall, 60% in winter.
